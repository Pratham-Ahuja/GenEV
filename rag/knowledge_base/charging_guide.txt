================================================================
CHARGING FUNDAMENTALS
================================================================

HOW EV CHARGING WORKS:
Electric vehicles store energy in battery packs.
Charging replenishes this energy from the electrical grid.
The process converts AC grid power to DC battery power.

KEY CHARGING TERMS:

kW (Kilowatt):
- Charging SPEED or POWER
- Higher kW = faster charging
- Example: 7.2 kW charger is faster than 3.3 kW

kWh (Kilowatt-hour):
- Charging CAPACITY or ENERGY
- Battery size measured in kWh
- Example: Nexon EV has 40.5 kWh battery

C-Rate:
- Charging speed relative to battery size
- 1C = charge full battery in 1 hour
- 0.5C = charge in 2 hours
- 2C = charge in 30 minutes
- Lower C-rate = gentler on battery

SoC (State of Charge):
- Current battery level as percentage
- 0% = empty, 100% = full
- Charging speed slows above 80% SoC

================================================================
TYPES OF EV CHARGING
================================================================

LEVEL 1 — SLOW AC CHARGING (2.2-3.3 kW)
Also called: Trickle charging, domestic charging

How it works:
- Uses standard 15A household socket (5-pin plug)
- Converts 230V AC to DC via onboard charger
- Slowest charging method

Speed:
- 3.3 kW charger: Adds ~15 km range per hour
- Full charge: 8-12 hours depending on battery size

Best for:
- Overnight home charging
- Low daily mileage users (<50 km/day)
- Backup charging option

Cost in India:
- No charger hardware cost (uses existing socket)
- Electricity cost: Rs 5-8 per unit (state dependent)
- Per km cost: Rs 0.8-1.2

LEVEL 2 — FAST AC CHARGING (7.2-22 kW)
Also called: Home fast charging, public AC charging

How it works:
- Requires dedicated charging unit (EVSE)
- Uses 3-phase power supply for higher speeds
- Onboard charger converts AC to DC

Speed:
- 7.2 kW: Adds ~35 km range per hour
- 11 kW: Adds ~50 km range per hour
- 22 kW: Adds ~90 km range per hour (if vehicle supports)
- Full charge: 3-6 hours

Connectors in India:
- Type 2 (Mennekes): Standard for 4-wheelers
- BS 1363: Standard UK-type plug (some models)

Best for:
- Home charging with dedicated setup
- Office charging during work hours
- Commercial premises

Cost:
- Charger hardware: Rs 15,000-50,000
- Installation: Rs 5,000-15,000
- Per unit: Rs 6-15 at public stations

LEVEL 3 — DC FAST CHARGING (25-350 kW)
Also called: DC fast charging, rapid charging

How it works:
- Bypasses onboard charger entirely
- Directly charges battery with DC power
- External high-power converter does AC-DC conversion
- Much faster but more hardware intensive

Speed by power level:
- 25 kW: 0-80% in ~90 minutes
- 50 kW: 0-80% in ~45-60 minutes
- 100 kW: 0-80% in ~25-30 minutes
- 150 kW: 0-80% in ~18-20 minutes
- 350 kW: 0-80% in ~8-10 minutes (ultra-premium EVs)

Why charging stops at 80%:
- Battery acceptance rate drops sharply above 80% SoC
- BMS throttles charging to protect cells
- Last 20% can take as long as first 80%
- Most public charging sessions target 80%

Connectors in India:
- CCS2 (Combined Charging System 2): Most common 4-wheeler
- CHAdeMO: Older standard (rare in India now)
- GB/T: Chinese standard (BYD vehicles)

Best for:
- Long distance travel
- Quick top-up during shopping/dining
- Emergency charging

Cost in India:
- Rs 15-25 per unit at public fast chargers
- Per km cost: Rs 2.5-4.5
- Session fees at some networks: Rs 25-50

================================================================
CHARGING CONNECTORS IN INDIA
================================================================

TYPE 2 (MENNEKES):
- Used for: AC Level 2 charging
- Compatible with: Most 4-wheel EVs in India
- Shape: Circular with 7 pins
- Maximum power: 22 kW (single phase limited to 7.4 kW)

CCS2 (COMBINED CHARGING SYSTEM 2):
- Used for: DC fast charging
- Compatible with: Nexon EV, Punch EV, MG Windsor, BYD Atto 3
- Shape: Type 2 connector with 2 extra DC pins below
- Maximum power: Up to 350 kW

CHAdeMO:
- Used for: DC fast charging (older standard)
- Compatible with: Older Nissan Leaf, some Japanese EVs
- Becoming obsolete in India
- Maximum power: Up to 100 kW

GB/T:
- Used for: BYD vehicles specifically
- Chinese standard
- Available at select BYD service centers
- Maximum power: Up to 120 kW

TYPE 1 (J1772):
- Older AC standard
- Rare in India
- Found on some imported used EVs

PUBLIC CHARGING NETWORKS IN INDIA
================================================================

TATA POWER EZ CHARGE:
- Largest network in India
- 4,000+ charging points
- Covers: Highways, malls, offices, apartments
- App: Tata Power EZ Charge
- Payment: App, RFID card
- Pricing: Rs 12-20 per unit (AC), Rs 18-25 per unit (DC)
- Best for: Tata EV owners (integrated in Nexon, Punch, Tiago)

CHARGE ZONE:
- 3,500+ charging points
- Strong highway coverage
- App: ChargeZone
- Pricing: Rs 10-22 per unit
- Notable: Highway charging stations with amenities

ATHER GRID:
- 1,800+ fast charging points
- Focus: Ather scooter users
- 1 kW fast chargers (proprietary Ather connector)
- Free charging: First 3 months for new Ather owners
- Locations: Malls, cafes, tech parks
- App: Ather app

FORTUM CHARGE & DRIVE:
- Highway fast charging focus
- 150+ stations on major highways
- CCS2 and CHAdeMO connectors
- Pricing: Rs 16-24 per unit

STATIQ:
- 3,000+ charging points
- Mix of AC and DC chargers
- App: Statiq
- Notable: Office complex focus
- Pricing: Rs 8-20 per unit

JIOEV (JIO-BP):
- Expanding rapidly (2024-2025)
- Highway and urban focus
- Pricing competitive: Rs 10-18 per unit

GOVERNMENT CHARGING (EESL):
- Energy Efficiency Services Limited
- 1,500+ stations
- Focus on government buildings, highways
- Lower pricing: Rs 8-15 per unit

HOME CHARGING SETUP — INDIA GUIDE
================================================================

REQUIREMENTS FOR HOME CHARGING:

APARTMENT DWELLERS:
Challenges:
- No dedicated parking with power access
- Society approval needed for installation
- Multiple EV owners create load management issues

Solutions:
1. Request society to install charging points in parking
2. Use portable EVSE (connects to regular socket)
3. Choose EV with good public charging network (Ather, Tata)
4. MG Windsor BaaS model reduces home charging dependency

Steps to get apartment charging:
1. Write formal request to society management
2. Present proposal with load calculations
3. Get electrical contractor quote
4. Obtain society NOC
5. Apply for load enhancement with DISCOM
6. Install dedicated circuit (separate meter recommended)

INDEPENDENT HOUSE:
Easiest setup:
1. Install 15A, 5-pin dedicated socket in garage
2. Get electrician to run dedicated circuit from DB
3. Use manufacturer-provided EVSE (usually included)
4. Optional: Upgrade to 7.2 kW wall box for faster charging

ELECTRICAL REQUIREMENTS:
- 15A socket: 3.3 kW charging (minimum)
- 32A circuit: 7.2 kW charging (recommended)
- 3-phase connection: 11-22 kW charging (premium setup)

LOAD CALCULATION:
- 3.3 kW charger: 14.3 amps on single phase
- 7.2 kW charger: 31.3 amps on single phase
- Ensure main fuse supports additional load
- Typical Indian home: 5-10 kW sanctioned load
- May need load enhancement application with DISCOM

COST OF HOME CHARGING SETUP:
- Basic (15A socket upgrade): Rs 2,000-5,000
- Dedicated circuit: Rs 5,000-10,000
- 7.2 kW wall box: Rs 15,000-30,000
- Installation: Rs 3,000-8,000
- Total typical setup: Rs 20,000-45,000

================================================================
CHARGING COST ANALYSIS — INDIA
================================================================

ELECTRICITY TARIFFS BY STATE (Domestic):
- Delhi: Rs 3-6 per unit (slab based)
- Maharashtra: Rs 4-9 per unit
- Karnataka: Rs 4-7 per unit
- Tamil Nadu: Rs 3.5-7 per unit
- Gujarat: Rs 3-6 per unit
- UP: Rs 3.5-6 per unit

COST PER KM CALCULATION:

Formula:
Cost per km = (Electricity rate × kWh per km) / Efficiency

Example — Nexon EV LR in Delhi:
- Battery: 40.5 kWh
- Real range: 320 km
- Consumption: 40.5/320 = 0.127 kWh per km
- Delhi electricity: Rs 4 per unit (domestic)
- Cost per km: 0.127 × 4 = Rs 0.51 per km

Comparison with Petrol car:
- Petrol car: 15 km/litre at Rs 95/litre = Rs 6.33 per km
- Nexon EV: Rs 0.51 per km (home charging)
- Saving: Rs 5.82 per km
- Annual saving (30 km/day): Rs 63,774

PUBLIC CHARGING COST:
- At Tata Power (DC fast): Rs 20/unit
- Nexon EV consumption: 0.127 kWh/km
- Cost per km: 0.127 × 20 = Rs 2.54 per km
- Still cheaper than petrol but much less than home charging

SMART CHARGING STRATEGIES
================================================================

TIME OF USE CHARGING:
- Charge at night (11 PM - 6 AM) for lower tariffs
- Some states offer off-peak EV tariffs
- Programme scheduled charging via vehicle app
- Benefits: Lower cost + less grid stress

SOLAR CHARGING:
- If you have rooftop solar: Near-zero cost charging
- Charge during daytime when solar generation peaks
- Bidirectional chargers (V2G): Coming to India in 2025-2026
- ROI: Solar system pays off in 4-6 years with EV charging

CHARGING ETIQUETTE AT PUBLIC STATIONS:
1. Move vehicle after charging completes
2. Do not occupy fast charger for slow charging needs
3. Plan charging during off-peak hours when possible
4. Keep charging cables tidy after use
5. Report faulty chargers via app immediately

TRIP PLANNING WITH CHARGING:
1. Plan charging stops every 200-250 km on highway
2. Use apps: PlugShare, Tata EZ Charge, ChargeZone
3. Target 20% SoC at charging stop (comfortable buffer)
4. Charge to 80% for highway segment (faster charging)
5. Charge to 100% only if needed for final destination

================================================================
CHARGING MISTAKES TO AVOID
================================================================

1. ALWAYS CHARGING TO 100%:
   - Problem: Keeps battery at high SoC constantly
   - Accelerates degradation (especially NMC)
   - Solution: Set charge limit to 80% for daily use
   - Exception: Long trips requiring full range

2. LETTING BATTERY GO TO 0%:
   - Problem: Deep discharge damages cells
   - BMS protects by cutting off at 5-10% displayed
   - Solution: Charge when battery reaches 20%

3. USING DC FAST CHARGING DAILY:
   - Problem: Heat generation accelerates degradation
   - Multiple fast charges per day compounds damage
   - Solution: Use AC home charging primarily

4. CHARGING IMMEDIATELY AFTER FAST DRIVING:
   - Problem: Battery already hot from driving
   - Adding charge heat compounds thermal stress
   - Solution: Wait 15-20 minutes for battery to cool

5. IGNORING CHARGING ERRORS:
   - Problem: Error codes indicate real issues
   - Ignoring can worsen battery condition
   - Solution: Check app for error codes, visit service center

6. USING UNVERIFIED CHARGERS:
   - Problem: Cheap/unverified chargers may not regulate properly
   - Risk of overcharging or incorrect voltage
   - Solution: Use certified chargers from reputed networks

================================================================
FUTURE OF EV CHARGING — INDIA
================================================================

VEHICLE TO GRID (V2G):
- EV battery supplies power back to grid
- Coming to India: 2025-2026
- Benefits: Earn money from stored energy
- First models: Mahindra XEV 9e supports V2L (Vehicle to Load)

VEHICLE TO LOAD (V2L):
- Power household appliances from EV battery
- Already available: Mahindra XEV 9e
- Power output: Up to 3.3 kW
- Use case: Power outages, camping, emergency power

BATTERY SWAPPING:
- Swap depleted battery for charged one in minutes
- Currently: Mainly for 2-wheelers (Bounce, Yulu)
- 4-wheeler swapping: Still emerging in India
- Challenge: Battery standardization across manufacturers

WIRELESS CHARGING:
- Charge without physical cable connection
- Still in development phase globally
- Expected in premium EVs by 2027-2028
- Efficiency: ~85-90% (slightly less than wired)

ULTRA-FAST CHARGING (350 kW+):
- 10 minute charges becoming possible
- Requires 800V architecture (Mahindra XEV 9e ready)
- Grid infrastructure challenge in India
- Expected mainstream: 2026-2028
